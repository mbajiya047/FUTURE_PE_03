### 1. Day of Surgery: What to Expect in the Chair
The actual surgical appointment is straightforward:
1. **Painless Anesthesia:** Gentle local anesthesia is administered. The operative area is completely numb; you feel slight vibration and water irrigation, but zero sharp pain.
2. **Precision Placement:** Utilizing a custom 3D-printed surgical stent, your surgeon prepares the osteotomy and inserts the sterile fixture into the jawbone.
3. **Provisional Tooth:** For teeth in the aesthetic smile zone, an immediate temporary crown is secured, ensuring you never walk out of our clinic with a visible gap.

Most patients return to light desk work and normal routines within **24 hours**.

---

### 2. The Critical Healing Window: Osseointegration Explained
Osseointegration is the biological marvel discovered by Swedish orthopedic surgeon Dr. Per-Ingvar Brånemark. Living osteoblasts (bone-building cells) deposit new bone matrix directly into the microscopic pores of the titanium implant.

* **Lower Jaw (Mandible):** Denser cortical bone; osseointegrates reliably in **8 to 12 weeks**.
* **Upper Jaw (Maxilla):** Softer, more cancellous bone; typically requires **12 to 16 weeks**.

During this phase, following strict hygiene and diet protocols is paramount. Review our comprehensive [dental implant recovery and aftercare guide](/dental-implant-aftercare-guide) for specific dietary phases.

"Same-day dental implants" are possible when specific criteria are satisfied:
* Primary insertion torque reaches **≥35 Ncm** (firm biological anchoring in native bone).
* The patient does not suffer from active uncontrolled bruxism (severe nighttime grinding).
* A temporary aesthetic crown is adjusted out of occlusal contact (the top tooth does not strike it while chewing).

# ARTICLE 3: Are Dental Implants Safe? Success Rates, Risks & Candidacy

> **Slug:** `/are-dental-implants-safe`

### Featured Snippet Summary
> **Are dental implants safe?**  
> Yes, dental implants are recognized globally as the safest, most predictable minor surgical procedure in modern dentistry, with a documented **clinical success rate of 95% to 98% over 10 to 20 years**. Adverse events are rare and preventable through proper pre-surgical 3D CBCT planning, medical screening, and stringent clinical sterilization protocols.

---

### Introduction
Undergoing any minor oral surgery can cause natural apprehension. Patients frequently ask: *"Will my body reject the implant?", "Is it safe if I have diabetes?", "What happens if an implant fails?"*

Titanium has been used in human orthopedic and dental medicine for over 50 years. It is completely bio-inert, non-toxic, and does not trigger allergic or immunological rejection responses in human tissue.

As detailed in our cornerstone guide to [dental implants in Bangalore](/dental-implants-bangalore), modern surgical techniques have made implantology safer and more predictable than standard root canal re-treatments.

---

### 1. The Real Success Rate: What Clinical Data Proves
Decades of peer-reviewed international dental research confirm:
* **Single Tooth Implants:** 98.2% 10-year survival rate.
* **Full-Arch Restorations (All-on-4 / All-on-6):** 97.4% 10-year survival rate.
* **Long-Term Longevity:** Over 85% of implants placed remain fully functional at 25+ years.

By contrast, traditional dental bridges carry a 10-year failure rate of 20% to 30%, largely due to secondary decay on the crowned supporting natural teeth.

---

### 2. Can Diabetic Patients Safely Receive Implants?
Yes. Millions of patients with Type 2 diabetes successfully receive dental implants every year.
* **Controlled Diabetes (HbA1c < 7.0%):** Success rates are identical to non-diabetic controls (96%–98%).
* **Moderately Controlled (HbA1c 7.0% – 8.0%):** Safe with specialized antibiotic prophylaxis and extended osseointegration timelines.
* **Uncontrolled (HbA1c > 8.5%):** Surgical placement should be temporarily deferred until blood glucose levels stabilize under physician supervision.

---

### 3. What Causes the 2%–3% of Implant Failures?
True biological "rejection" does not occur. When an implant fails, it is almost always attributable to one of three issues:
1. **Early Failure (Failure to Osseointegrate):** Caused by premature heavy chewing loads during weeks 1–6, excessive surgical heat, or micro-movement exceeding 150 microns.
2. **Late Failure (Peri-Implantitis):** Caused by chronic bacterial plaque buildup under the gumline, analogous to gum disease on a natural tooth.
3. **Severe Untreated Bruxism:** Heavy nighttime clenching without a protective nightguard can mechanically stress the bone-fixture interface.

Understanding proper post-operative cleaning is the single best defense against peri-implantitis; consult our [dental implant recovery and aftercare guide](/dental-implant-aftercare-guide) for daily cleaning checklists.

### Introduction
The day of your implant surgery is just the beginning of your journey toward a lifelong smile. While the surgical fixture is placed by your oral surgeon, **long-term success depends largely on your home recovery habits during the first 14 days**.

Following these evidence-based aftercare instructions will prevent post-operative infection, minimize discomfort, and ensure rapid, stable osseointegration.

---

### 1. The First 24–48 Hours: Critical Rules
* **Firm Gauze Pressure:** Keep the sterile gauze pad gently clenched over the surgical site for 45 to 60 minutes post-surgery to promote stable blood clot formation.
* **Do NOT Spit or Use Straws:** Creating negative oral suction can dislodge the fragile blood clot, causing localized bleeding and delaying tissue healing.
* **Cold Therapy:** Apply an ice pack to the external cheek near the surgical site (15 minutes on, 15 minutes off) during the first 24 hours to drastically reduce swelling.
* **Prescription Medications:** Take your prescribed pain relievers and complete your full course of antibiotics as directed by your surgeon.

---

### 2. Dietary Guidelines: What to Eat & What to Avoid

```
┌───────────────────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Recommended Soft Foods (Days 1–7)             │ Foods to STRICTLY Avoid (Days 1–14)           │
├───────────────────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Dhal khichdi, curd rice (room temperature)    │ Hard nuts, seeds, popcorn kernels             │
│ Blended fruit smoothies (drunk from glass)    │ Spicy Indian curries / acidic citrus juices   │
│ Mashed potatoes, soft scrambled eggs          │ Crusty breads, tough meats, chewy dosas       │
│ Clear vegetable or chicken broths             │ Piping hot coffee, tea, or scalding soups     │
│ Paneer bhurji, yogurt, soft ice creams        │ Chewing directly on the surgical implant site │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

After 14 days, you can gradually reintroduce firmer foods as your gum tissue seals and comfort permits.

---

### 3. Oral Hygiene Protocol: How to Clean Your New Implant

* **Days 1–3:** Do not brush directly over the fresh surgical incisions. Brush remaining natural teeth carefully.
* **Warm Salt Water Rinses:** Starting 24 hours post-surgery, dissolve 1/2 teaspoon of table salt in a cup of lukewarm water and rinse very gently after meals.
* **Chlorhexidine Mouthwash:** If prescribed, swish 0.12% Chlorhexidine gluconate mouthwash twice daily for 30 seconds to suppress bacterial biofilms without burning.
* **Long-Term Hygiene (Post-Crown Placement):**
  * Use an ultra-soft or sonic electric toothbrush with non-abrasive fluoride toothpaste.
  * Incorporate an interdental brush (plastic-coated wire) or a **Water Flosser** to flush debris from beneath the prosthetic crown contours.

---

### 4. Warning Signs: When to Call the Clinic
While minor swelling and mild tenderness are completely normal, contact our emergency line if you experience:
* Continuous bleeding that saturates gauze after 4 hours of direct biting pressure.
* Severe throbbing pain unmanaged by prescribed analgesics after 72 hours.
* Persistent numbness in the lower lip, chin, or tongue lasting longer than 12 hours.
* Noticeable loosening or mobility of the temporary abutment or fixture.
